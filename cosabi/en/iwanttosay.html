# Developer's Note: From a Father's "Goldfish Brain" I am a **Christian**, an **independent software developer**, and a father of three (oh, and a cat). Back when I was single and under my parents' wing, the little details of life were never a problem. But ever since I started a family and moved out on my own, reality has kept piling on: someone has to keep an eye on all the household supplies, and I had to learn to watch seasonal price swings just to fight inflation... for my limited brain capacity, that was a massive challenge. ### Admitting the Problem Is the First Step to Solving It Before writing the first line of code, I honestly faced my two pain points: 1. **Losing track of time**: Was this toothbrush changed three months ago, or four? I truly can't remember. 2. **Price disconnection**: Standing in front of a supermarket shelf, I can't recall what the same item cost three months ago, so I can't tell if today's price is a good one. These are really the same problem: **what I need isn't a better memory, but a place that remembers "how long it's been" and "what it cost back then" for me.** If my brain can't hold it, let a tool hold it. --- ## Development Journey: Purity After Subtraction The original plan was grand. I wanted to build a "super app" tying together storage, prices, and finances, and even planned to connect to the invoice carrier system. But I hit a wall during research: the government had tightened API access for independent developers, and one of the pillars I had imagined simply broke. After the first version launched, I found another problem: **the item warehouse asked for so much setup that even I didn't want to fill it in.** I ended up going back to the most primitive way, jotting things down line by line in a notes app to cover the gaps in my memory. That's when I understood the problem wasn't too few features. I had built something I didn't want to use myself. So I started over, cut everything, and **kept only the two problems I actually run into every day**. What remains in this version is "Tracking" and "Prices." --- ## What It Does for You ### Tracking: How long has it been? * Note the moment you started something, and the app works out how long it's been. * Check in whenever you like: snap a photo, jot a note. Every check-in stays in the history, along with how long it's been since the previous one. * You decide whether each check-in restarts the timer (it does by default), and you can set a reminder. * Good for: toothbrushes, water filters, A/C filters, the cat's litter box, medicine expiry dates, kids' vaccines and checkups... anything where you think "I roughly remember, but I'm not sure." ### Prices: Is this a good price? * Snap the price tag at the store. The app reads the product name and price, then files it under an existing item or creates a new one. * For each item, you can see: * How today's price compares with **the same month in the past**: higher, lower, or about the same * **Monthly price patterns**: which month is the cheapest time to buy * **Prices by store**: where it's cheaper * Is a "pack of 3" cheaper than buying singles? Enter the pack price and quantity, and the app converts it to an average per piece so different packages can be compared side by side. The screen still shows exactly what you entered. * Organize items with tags that can be nested (for example, "Fruit › Mango"). It works like folders, so things don't get messier as they grow. --- ## Principles I Want to Hold On To * **Build only what I use myself.** If I wouldn't open it every day, it doesn't belong. * **The simpler, the better.** Logging an entry must be faster than jotting it in a notes app, or the app has no reason to exist. * **Grow slowly.** There is deliberately no AI for now. Once I've used the app myself, feel I really need it, and know others would too, I'll add it back little by little. --- ## One Last Thing Cosabi is made for people like me: people whose days are packed with chores, kids, and work, whose heads have no spare room left, and who still want to live carefully and keep things in order. It isn't perfect or fancy, but it's something I actually use every day. If it happens to help you too, that would make me very happy. Any suggestions or thoughts are always welcome.
